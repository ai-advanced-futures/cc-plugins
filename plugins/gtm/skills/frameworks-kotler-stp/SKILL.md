# Kotler STP Framework

## Purpose

Kotler's STP is the foundational GTM discipline that prevents the most common go-to-market failure: trying to be everything to everyone. It forces a deliberate sequence — first divide the total addressable market into distinct segments, then select the segment(s) where you have the greatest right to win, then craft a positioning statement that articulates a singular, defensible benefit in that segment's language. STP is not a one-time exercise; it should be revisited whenever entering a new market, launching a new product, or when growth stalls.

## Core Concepts

**Segmentation** — The act of dividing a heterogeneous market into homogeneous groups whose members share similar needs, behaviors, or characteristics. Segmentation variables include:
- **Demographic:** company size, industry, revenue band, job title, geography
- **Psychographic:** values, risk tolerance, innovation appetite, organizational culture
- **Behavioral:** purchase frequency, use case, switching cost, buyer journey stage
- **Needs-based (preferred):** grouped by the job-to-be-done or pain they share, regardless of demographic profile

**Targeting** — Evaluating each segment against four criteria and selecting where to compete:
1. **Size** — Is the segment large enough to be commercially meaningful?
2. **Accessibility** — Can you reach this segment through available channels?
3. **Profitability** — Does the segment's willingness-to-pay support unit economics?
4. **Fit** — Does your product's core strength match this segment's primary pain?

**Positioning** — The deliberate act of occupying a distinctive place in the target segment's mind. Effective positioning is specific, believable, and difficult for competitors to replicate. It is not a tagline; it is a strategic statement that guides all downstream messaging, pricing, and channel decisions.

## Application Protocol

**Step 1 — Segment the Market**
1. List all possible customer types currently buying or who could buy in the category.
2. Apply segmentation variables; group customers whose needs are materially similar.
3. Name each segment concisely (e.g., "Mid-market RevOps teams," "Bootstrapped DTC founders").
4. Estimate segment size (accounts) and approximate ACV per segment.

**Step 2 — Score and Select Target Segment(s)**
Score each segment 1-3 on Size, Accessibility, Profitability, and Fit. Sum scores. The highest-scoring segment(s) are primary targets. Resist targeting more than two segments in an early-stage GTM — focus compounds.

**Step 3 — Develop Positioning Statement**
Use the canonical Kotler template:

> "For [target segment], [product name] is the [category frame] that [primary benefit] because [key proof point]."

Fill each slot with specificity:
- **Target segment:** precise description (not "enterprises")
- **Category frame:** the competitive set you want to be evaluated against
- **Primary benefit:** the single most important outcome for this segment
- **Key proof point:** the mechanism or evidence that makes the benefit credible

**Step 4 — Validate the Positioning**
- Does the category frame help or hurt? (e.g., "data warehouse" vs. "operational analytics layer")
- Is the benefit unique to you, or do three competitors claim the same thing?
- Does the proof point survive a skeptic's challenge?
- Would your target segment immediately recognize themselves in the "For [target]" slot?

**Step 5 — Cascade the Positioning**
The positioning statement becomes the source of truth for: homepage headline, sales deck opening slide, outbound messaging, ad copy, and PR angle. Every downstream artifact should be traceable back to this statement.

```
SEGMENTATION MAP
----------------
Segment Name | Size (accounts) | Est. ACV | Pain Profile
[Segment A]  | [X]             | [$Y]     | [primary pain]
[Segment B]  | [X]             | [$Y]     | [primary pain]

SEGMENT SCORING
---------------
Segment | Size | Accessibility | Profitability | Fit | Total
[A]     |  3   |      2        |       3       |  3  |  11
[B]     |  2   |      3        |       2       |  2  |   9

SELECTED TARGET: [Segment A] — rationale: [1-sentence explanation]

POSITIONING STATEMENT
---------------------
For [target segment],
[Product] is the [category]
that [primary benefit]
because [proof point].

VALIDATION FLAGS
----------------
[ ] Benefit is unique (not claimed by 3+ competitors)
[ ] Category frame favors our strengths
[ ] Proof point is specific and verifiable
[ ] Target segment recognizes themselves in the statement
```
