# Moral Marketing Principles

## Purpose

This plugin writes marketing that the product can prove. Marketing that a reader can audit is marketing that a reader can trust.

## The Five Moral Marketing Principles

### 1. Every Claim Is Traceable to Evidence
Every product claim must be linkable to a specific evidence source: a feature in the codebase, a customer outcome, a roadmap commitment with budget and exec sponsor, or a documented vision. Claims without evidence are Tier 5 — blocked.

### 2. No Manufactured Urgency
Use scarcity and deadlines only when the limit is real and documented, for example "Act now — limited time offer!" with a documented end date. A false limit harms the customer and costs trust when the customer finds it.

### 3. No Manufactured Social Proof
Use only real, verifiable proof points: real testimonials, true user counts, and customer logos with permission. A fake proof point, once found, discredits every other claim.

### 4. Competitor References Must Be Factual
Base comparative claims on publicly verifiable facts, and cite a source for each competitor weakness, because an unsourced comparison is a legal risk. Constructive tension (Challenger Sale) is permitted.

### 5. Emotional Appeals Must Be Grounded in Reality
Ground each emotional appeal in a real customer outcome. Fear or aspiration that outpaces what the product delivers is manufactured.

## The Moral Marketing Checklist

Before any artifact is delivered, verify:

- [ ] All capability claims have a Truth Tier ≥ 1 and are evidenced
- [ ] All quantified claims (%, Nx) have an in-line source
- [ ] No manufactured urgency or false scarcity
- [ ] All social proof (logos, testimonials, counts) is real and verifiable
- [ ] Competitor references cite public sources
- [ ] Emotional appeals are grounded in documented customer outcomes
- [ ] No Tier 5 claims appear anywhere in the artifact

A "no" on any item = artifact must be revised before delivery.
