# Golden Circle (Sinek)

## Purpose

Simon Sinek's Golden Circle reverses the conventional marketing logic of leading with product features (What) or process (How) and instead demands that all messaging begin with purpose (Why). The neurological argument is precise: the neocortex (rational brain) processes What and How; the limbic brain (which controls decision-making and behavior) processes Why. Great GTM does not sell products — it articulates a belief that the target buyer already holds, then positions the product as the obvious vehicle for acting on that belief. This framework validates whether your messaging is leading with Why and audits all downstream GTM surfaces for compliance.

## Core Concepts

**The Three Rings**

- **Why** — The innermost ring. The purpose, cause, or belief that drives the organization. Why does the company exist beyond making money? Why should anyone care? The Why is about the change the company believes in and what it is fighting for, not about the product. Authentic Why statements are in short supply and are extremely difficult to copy because they must be true.

- **How** — The middle ring. The distinctive methods, principles, or values that bring the Why to life. The How differentiates you from others who share your Why. It is the operating model that makes the belief actionable.

- **What** — The outermost ring. The tangible products, services, and features that are the evidence of Why and How. The What is the easiest thing for competitors to replicate.

**The Critical Inversion**
Most companies communicate outside-in: "Here is what we do, here is how we do it better, and here is why you should care." Buyers experience this as a features pitch and engage their rational brain to comparison-shop. Companies that lead inside-out — Why first — engage the limbic brain, trigger a feeling of alignment and trust, then give the rational brain evidence to justify a decision already made emotionally.

**Why vs. Mission Statement**
Most mission statements describe What or How with aspirational language. A true Why is a belief about the world: "We believe [X]" where X is a conviction about how things should be that the target buyer shares. Test: does the Why statement work even if the product did not exist? If yes, it is a real Why. If no, it is a product description in disguise.

**People Buy Why, Not What**
The proof point: Apple's Why ("We challenge the status quo") is why a computer company successfully sells music players, phones, and watches. The consistent Why enables category expansion because buyers follow the belief, not the SKU. GTM that leads with Why builds a tribe; GTM that leads with What builds a customer list.

## Application Protocol

**Step 1 — Extract the Why**
Answer the following in sequence:
1. Why did the founders start this company — what did they believe was wrong with the world?
2. What would be lost if this company disappeared tomorrow (not its product, its belief)?
3. Complete the stem: "We believe [conviction about the world that our buyers also hold]."
4. Does this Why apply to employees who joined for reasons beyond compensation?

If the Why statement contains product language, product benefits, or competitive comparisons — it is not the Why. Rewrite until it stands alone as a conviction.

**Step 2 — Articulate the How**
Identify 2-3 distinctive operating principles that make the Why real:
- How does the company make decisions when faced with tradeoffs?
- What would the company refuse to do even if it were profitable?
- What is structurally different about how the product delivers on the belief?

**Step 3 — Define the What**
Only after Why and How are clear, describe the product as evidence: "And the proof that we believe [Why] is that we built [What]."

**Step 4 — Audit All GTM Surfaces for Why Leadership**
Review: homepage headline, homepage subheadline, first slide of sales deck, outbound email opening sentence, investor pitch opening, press releases, social bio, job descriptions.

For each surface, ask:
- Does this lead with Why (belief/conviction) or What (feature/capability)?
- Would a reader feel aligned with a belief before they understand the product?
- Is this surface communicating inside-out or outside-in?

Flag all surfaces that lead with What or How. Rewrite to lead with Why.

**Step 5 — Validate Why Authenticity**
The Why must survive three tests:
1. **Employee test:** Do employees spontaneously use Why language when describing the company to friends?
2. **Customer test:** Do customers ever describe why they chose you in belief language, not feature language?
3. **Competitive test:** Could a competitor credibly steal your Why? If yes, it is not specific enough or not true enough.

**Step 6 — Apply Why to Messaging Architecture**
Structure all major messaging assets in Golden Circle order:
- Opening: Why (belief statement — 1-2 sentences)
- Middle: How (distinctive approach — 2-3 sentences)
- Close: What (product evidence — specific, 1-2 sentences)
- Call to action: invite into the belief, not just the product
