# MEDDPICC

## Purpose

MEDDPICC is the enterprise sales qualification framework that separates opportunities that will close from opportunities that will slip. It was developed at PTC in the 1990s and has since become the standard qualification methodology for complex, multi-stakeholder B2B sales. Its power is in rigor: it forces sellers and sales managers to assess eight specific dimensions of deal health, score each on a 0-2 scale, and make a binary forecastability decision based on the aggregate score. Only deals at or above the forecastability threshold (12/16) enter commit forecast, because deals below it are at risk of endless slippage. MEDDPICC also provides the action map: for every gap in a dimension, there is a specific selling action to close it.

## Core Concepts

**The 8 MEDDPICC Dimensions**

**M — Metrics**
The quantified business impact the customer expects from the purchase. Metrics must be specific, agreed-upon, and owned by the economic buyer. "Improved efficiency" is not a metric. "Reduce time-to-close from 47 to 30 days, saving $2.4M in rep capacity annually" is a metric.

Score 0: No metrics defined  |  Score 1: Metrics exist but not validated by economic buyer  |  Score 2: Specific, quantified metrics agreed with economic buyer

**E — Economic Buyer**
The single person with budget authority and final decision power. Not the champion, not the committee — the one person who can say yes when everyone else says no. The economic buyer must be directly accessed — not accessed through a champion relay.

Score 0: Economic buyer unidentified  |  Score 1: Identified but not yet directly accessed  |  Score 2: Directly accessed, metrics discussed, sponsorship confirmed

**D — Decision Criteria**
The formal and informal criteria the organization will use to make the purchase decision. Technical criteria, commercial criteria, legal criteria, political criteria. Without knowledge of how they will decide, you cannot ensure you score highest on their evaluation.

Score 0: Criteria unknown  |  Score 1: Partial criteria known (technical only, no commercial/political)  |  Score 2: All criteria known — formal and informal — confirmed with multiple stakeholders

**D — Decision Process**
The specific steps the organization will follow to make and ratify the decision: who participates, in what sequence, with what approvals, by what date. Procurement timelines, legal review cycles, and board approval requirements are part of the process.

Score 0: Process unknown  |  Score 1: High-level process known, details unclear  |  Score 2: Detailed process mapped with specific steps, owners, and dates confirmed

**P — Paper Process**
The contractual, legal, and procurement mechanics that must complete before a deal closes. Master Service Agreement, security review, data processing agreement, legal redlines, purchase order generation. Paper process issues are the most common cause of late-stage deal slippage. Paper process must be mapped and started early.

Score 0: Paper process not discussed  |  Score 1: High-level awareness only (e.g., "we have standard procurement")  |  Score 2: Specific documents required and timelines known; process started or scheduled

**I — Identify Pain**
The specific, urgent business problem driving the purchase. Pain must be at a level of urgency that creates compelling event — the customer must feel they cannot afford to delay. Pain must be connected to the metrics (M) and validated by the economic buyer (E).

Score 0: Pain not clearly identified or not urgent  |  Score 1: Pain identified but not quantified or not urgent  |  Score 2: Specific, urgent, quantified pain confirmed with economic buyer

**C — Champion**
A person inside the buying organization who has power and influence AND is actively working for your win — in rooms you cannot enter, to people you have not met. A champion is not a fan or a friendly contact. The Champion test determines real championship.

Score 0: No champion identified  |  Score 1: Friendly contact who has not demonstrated selling behavior  |  Score 2: Champion has passed the Champion test (see below)

**C — Competition**
The full competitive landscape: other vendors being evaluated, internal build options, the status quo ("do nothing"), and internal resource reallocation. You must know who you are competing against and what their strengths are in this specific deal.

Score 0: Competition unknown  |  Score 1: Vendors identified but not mapped to decision criteria  |  Score 2: Full competitive picture known, your differentiation mapped to their criteria, and competitive traps set or avoided

**The Champion Test**
A champion must be verified — not assumed. The Champion test:
1. Ask your champion to provide intelligence you could not get yourself (org chart, private decision criteria, competitor bid prices, economic buyer's actual priorities).
2. Ask your champion to set up a meeting with the economic buyer that they facilitate.
3. Ask your champion to advocate for your solution in a forum where you are not present and report back on the conversation.

If the champion does all three: Champion confirmed. If they claim to support you but refuse or are unable to do any of these: they are a friendly contact, not a champion. Treat as Score 1.

**The Forecastability Gate**
- Score ≥ 12/16 → Deal is forecastable (can enter commit or best case forecast)
- Score 10-11/16 → Deal is upside / pipeline — requires specific gap-closing actions before promotion to commit
- Score < 10/16 → Deal is at risk or early stage — should not be in near-term forecast at all

**Scoring Matrix**

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| M — Metrics | None defined | Exists, not EB-validated | Specific, EB-agreed |
| E — Economic Buyer | Unidentified | Identified, not accessed | Accessed, sponsorship confirmed |
| D — Decision Criteria | Unknown | Partial (technical only) | All criteria known |
| D — Decision Process | Unknown | High-level only | Steps, owners, dates confirmed |
| P — Paper Process | Not discussed | Aware but not mapped | Documents and timeline started |
| I — Identify Pain | Unclear / not urgent | Identified, not quantified | Specific, urgent, EB-confirmed |
| C — Champion | None | Friendly, unverified | Champion test passed |
| C — Competition | Unknown | Vendors known, not mapped | Full picture, differentiation set |

## Application Protocol

**Step 1 — Score Every Active Opportunity**
Apply MEDDPICC scoring at every forecast review. Move deals below the 12/16 threshold out of commit forecast.

**Step 2 — Identify Gaps**
For every dimension scored below 2, classify as a gap. Rank gaps by urgency: which missing dimension most directly threatens the deal?

**Step 3 — Assign Gap-Closing Actions**
For each gap, there is a specific action:
- M gap: Schedule working session with economic buyer to build the business case together
- E gap: Request an executive-level meeting through the champion; the deal advances only after this meeting
- D (Criteria) gap: Ask champion for the evaluation scorecard or RFP criteria; set up a technical deep-dive to define criteria collaboratively
- D (Process) gap: Request a "decision process walk-through" meeting; ask who needs to sign off and by when
- P gap: Introduce your legal/procurement team to theirs; start MSA and security review early
- I gap: Run a discovery session focused on consequence quantification (SPIN implication questions)
- C (Champion) gap: Run the Champion test; escalate directly to EB if champion cannot open doors
- C (Competition) gap: Run a competitive landscape review; adjust your differentiation narrative to score highest on their actual criteria

**Step 4 — Set Mutually Agreed Close Date**
A close date that only you know is not a close date — it is a guess. The MEDDPICC close date must be mutually agreed with the economic buyer and anchored to their decision process timeline, not your quota deadline.

**Step 5 — Weekly Deal Review Cadence**
In every deal review: score the deal on all 8 dimensions, identify what changed since last week, and confirm the gap-closing actions from the previous week were executed. Deals that cannot show forward progress on gaps should be downgraded in forecast.
