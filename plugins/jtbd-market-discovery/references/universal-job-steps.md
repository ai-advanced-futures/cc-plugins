# Universal Job Steps Framework

**Author:** Luis F. Amadeo (Chief Agentic Officer)
**Framework:** Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI) - Tony Ulwick
**Purpose:** Systematic job decomposition for customer need generation

---

## Overview

The Universal Job Steps framework is a fundamental component of Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI). It provides a structured approach to breaking down any job into its constituent steps, enabling systematic identification of customer needs.

**Core Insight:** All jobs, regardless of industry or domain, follow the same universal pattern of 6 phases.

---

## The 6 Universal Phases

### 1. UNDERSTAND Phase

**Purpose:** Gather information needed to do the job

**What happens in this phase:**
- Research available options
- Identify requirements and constraints
- Assess current resources and capabilities
- Estimate time, cost, and effort needed
- Compare alternatives
- Verify information accuracy

**Example steps (Navigation):**
- Estimate departure time
- Determine what needs to be prepared

**Example steps (Team Coverage):**
- Identify upcoming absence requests
- Determine team capacity during absence period
- Assess criticality of projects during that time

**Common ACTION verbs:**
- Determine, Identify, Locate, Assess, Measure, Estimate, Compare, Verify, Research, Evaluate

---

### 2. PLAN Phase

**Purpose:** Prepare for job execution

**What happens in this phase:**
- Define the approach or strategy
- Establish goals and success criteria
- Prepare resources and materials
- Organize tasks and sequence
- Prioritize activities
- Schedule timeline
- Allocate resources

**Example steps (Navigation):**
- Plan the stops
- Set the departure time

**Example steps (Team Coverage):**
- Identify potential backup resources
- Create coverage schedule
- Communicate absence to stakeholders

**Common ACTION verbs:**
- Define, Establish, Prepare, Organize, Prioritize, Sequence, Allocate, Schedule, Plan, Arrange

---

### 3. EXECUTE Phase

**Purpose:** Perform the core job activities

**What happens in this phase:**
- Take action according to plan
- Apply the solution or approach
- Implement the strategy
- Perform the main tasks
- Follow the established process
- Maintain focus on goals

**Example steps (Navigation):**
- Get to the vehicle
- Prepare the vehicle
- Travel to the destination
- Make planned stops

**Example steps (Team Coverage):**
- Approve absence request
- Assign backup resources to critical tasks
- Update project schedules
- Monitor coverage effectiveness

**Common ACTION verbs:**
- Minimize, Maximize, Reduce, Increase, Ensure, Avoid, Maintain, Achieve, Execute, Perform

---

### 4. ASSESS Phase

**Purpose:** Evaluate progress and results

**What happens in this phase:**
- Measure outcomes against goals
- Check quality of results
- Verify completion of tasks
- Confirm requirements are met
- Detect problems or gaps
- Monitor progress indicators
- Track performance metrics

**Example steps (Navigation):**
- Assess when the destination will be reached

**Example steps (Team Coverage):**
- Verify critical tasks are covered
- Check for any emerging gaps
- Confirm backup resources are adequate

**Common ACTION verbs:**
- Measure, Evaluate, Verify, Confirm, Detect, Monitor, Track, Validate, Check, Review

---

### 5. REVISE Phase

**Purpose:** Make adjustments based on assessment

**What happens in this phase:**
- Correct errors or problems
- Adjust approach based on feedback
- Optimize performance
- Improve efficiency or quality
- Refine the solution
- Update plans or strategies
- Modify parameters

**Example steps (Navigation):**
- Reset the route as needed

**Example steps (Team Coverage):**
- Reassign backup resources if gaps emerge
- Adjust project timelines if needed
- Update coverage schedule based on changes

**Common ACTION verbs:**
- Correct, Adjust, Optimize, Improve, Refine, Update, Modify, Adapt, Revise, Fine-tune

---

### 6. CONCLUDE Phase

**Purpose:** Finalize and close out the job

**What happens in this phase:**
- Complete final tasks
- Finalize deliverables
- Document results or learnings
- Store information for future use
- Archive materials
- Summarize outcomes
- Report completion
- Clean up resources

**Example steps (Navigation):**
- Park the vehicle
- Walk to the destination

**Example steps (Team Coverage):**
- Document coverage effectiveness
- Update absence records
- Store lessons learned for future planning
- Close out the absence request

**Common ACTION verbs:**
- Complete, Finalize, Document, Store, Archive, Summarize, Report, Close, Clean up

## How to Decompose Jobs into Steps

### Step 1: Start with the Main Job

Identify the main job to be done (outcome-focused, stable over time).

**Example:** "Get to destinations on time"

### Step 2: Walk Through the 6 Phases

For each phase, ask: "What specific steps must be taken in this phase?"

**UNDERSTAND:** What information do I need before I can start?
**PLAN:** Once I have that information, what preparation is needed?
**EXECUTE:** What are the core actions I take to do the job?
**ASSESS:** How do I evaluate if things are going well?
**REVISE:** What adjustments might I need to make?
**CONCLUDE:** How do I finalize and wrap up?

### Step 3: Generate 8-15 Total Steps

**Target:** 8-15 steps total across all 6 phases

**Distribution:** Not all phases will have the same number of steps. Typically:
- UNDERSTAND: 2-3 steps
- PLAN: 2-3 steps
- EXECUTE: 3-5 steps (usually the most)
- ASSESS: 1-2 steps
- REVISE: 1-2 steps
- CONCLUDE: 1-2 steps

### Step 4: Validate Your Steps

Check that each step is:
- ✅ **Specific** - Not vague or general
- ✅ **Actionable** - Describes something that can be done
- ✅ **Sequential** - Follows a logical order
- ✅ **Necessary** - Required for the job (not optional nice-to-haves)
- ✅ **Phase-appropriate** - Fits the phase it's in

## Common Patterns Across Jobs

### Pattern 1: Information → Action → Evaluation

All jobs follow this meta-pattern:
1. **UNDERSTAND & PLAN** = Information gathering and preparation
2. **EXECUTE** = Taking action
3. **ASSESS & REVISE** = Evaluation and adjustment
4. **CONCLUDE** = Finalization

### Pattern 2: Multiple Iterations

Some jobs iterate through ASSESS → REVISE → EXECUTE cycles multiple times before CONCLUDE.

**Example (Navigation):**
- ASSESS when destination will be reached
- REVISE route if traffic appears
- Continue EXECUTE (travel) with new route
- ASSESS again
- Repeat until arrival

### Pattern 3: Preparation-Heavy vs Execution-Heavy

**Preparation-heavy jobs** have many UNDERSTAND and PLAN steps:
- Research projects
- Strategic planning
- Product design

**Execution-heavy jobs** have many EXECUTE steps:
- Manufacturing
- Service delivery
- Routine operations

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 1: Solution-Focused Steps

**Bad:** "Use GPS app to find route"
**Good:** "Determine optimal route to destination"

**Why:** Solutions change, needs don't. GPS is a solution; determining routes is a need.

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 2: Vague Steps

**Bad:** "Do research"
**Good:** "Identify available options that meet requirements"

**Why:** Vague steps don't help generate specific customer needs.

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 3: Skipping Phases

**Bad:** Only listing EXECUTE steps, ignoring UNDERSTAND, PLAN, ASSESS, REVISE, CONCLUDE

**Why:** Jobs are comprehensive. Missing phases means missing customer needs and opportunities.

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 4: Too Granular

**Bad:** "Open vehicle door, Insert key, Turn key, Press brake pedal, Shift to drive..."
**Good:** "Prepare the vehicle"

**Why:** Too granular makes the job unmang able. Aim for 8-15 steps, not 50+.

### ❌ Anti-Pattern 5: Steps Out of Order

**Bad:** Putting "Assess results" before "Execute actions"

**Why:** Steps should follow the natural sequence of how the job is performed.

## Using Job Steps to Generate Customer Needs

Once you have job steps, use them as the foundation for generating customer needs:

**For each step:**
1. Identify VARIABLES in that step
2. Identify ACTIONS needed for those variables
3. Combine ACTION + VARIABLE = CUSTOMER NEED

**Example:**

**Job Step:** "Plan the stops" (Navigation)

**Variables:** Optimal sequence, Routes, Open times, Wait times, Parking

**Customer Needs:**
- Determine optimal sequence of stops
- Determine routes to complete errands
- Determine open times for locations
- Determine wait times at locations
- Determine parking availability for errands
- Reduce total number of stops needed
- Minimize additional time required for stops

**Result:** 7 customer needs from 1 job step × 15 steps = ~100 needs total

## Benefits of Universal Job Steps

### 1. Systematic Coverage

By following the 6 phases, you ensure comprehensive coverage of the entire job. Nothing is missed.

### 2. Repeatable Process

The framework works for any job in any industry. Once you learn it, you can apply it everywhere.

### 3. Generates 80-100 Needs

Job steps are the foundation for customer need generation. 8-15 steps × 6-10 needs per step = 80-100 needs.

### 4. Reveals Hidden Opportunities

By forcing decomposition into all 6 phases, you uncover needs in areas often overlooked (like ASSESS, REVISE, CONCLUDE).

### 5. Enables Segmentation

Different customer segments may prioritize different phases. Some care more about PLAN, others about EXECUTE, others about ASSESS.

## Best Practices

### ✅ Start Broad, Then Refine

First pass: identify rough steps in each phase
Second pass: refine wording to be specific and actionable
Third pass: validate sequence and completeness

### ✅ Use Active Verbs

Each step should start with or clearly imply an action verb:
- "Estimate departure time" (verb = estimate)
- "Plan the stops" (verb = plan)
- "Assess when destination will be reached" (verb = assess)

### ✅ Maintain Consistent Granularity

All steps should be at roughly the same level of detail. Don't mix high-level ("Execute the plan") with low-level ("Press the accelerator pedal").

### ✅ Validate with Customers

Once you have steps, validate with customers: "Does this match how you actually do this job?" They'll tell you if you missed something or got the sequence wrong.

### ✅ Don't Skip Phases

Even if a phase seems minor, include at least 1 step. Every phase has customer needs, and those needs might be opportunities.

## Summary

**Universal Job Steps Framework:**
- All jobs follow 6 phases: UNDERSTAND → PLAN → EXECUTE → ASSESS → REVISE → CONCLUDE
- Target: 8-15 total steps across the 6 phases
- Steps should be specific, actionable, sequential, and necessary
- Job steps are the foundation for generating 80-100 customer needs
- The framework is repeatable and works across all industries

**Why It Works:**
- Systematic and comprehensive (nothing is missed)
- Forces you to think through the entire job lifecycle
- Reveals needs in often-overlooked phases (ASSESS, REVISE, CONCLUDE)
- Provides structure for customer need generation
