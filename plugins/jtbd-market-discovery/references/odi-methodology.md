# Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI) Methodology

**Author:** Luis F. Amadeo (Chief Agentic Officer)
**Source:** Jobs To Be Done Theory and Methodology Course
**Framework:** Tony Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation
**Application:** Market opportunity discovery and customer need identification

---

## Core Principles

### Principle 1: Customers Don't Buy Products, They Hire Them

**The Insight:**
Your customers are not buying your product - they are hiring your product to get a job done.

**Classic Examples:**

**Example 1: The Drill and the Hole**
> "Customers do not want a quarter inch drill, they want a quarter inch hole."

- The PRODUCT: Quarter inch drill (changes over time - corded, cordless, battery-powered, etc.)
- The JOB TO BE DONE: "Creating a quarter inch hole" (stable, doesn't change)

**Example 2: Navigation Applications**
> "Customers do not want navigation applications, they want to get to destinations on time."

- The PRODUCT: Navigation applications (changes dramatically - paper maps, GPS devices, smartphone apps, etc.)
- The JOB TO BE DONE: "Getting to destinations on time" (stable across decades)

**Why This Matters:**
- PRODUCTS can change dramatically over time
- All PRODUCTS eventually go obsolete
- JOBS TO BE DONE are stable and don't change
- When defining your CUSTOMER PROBLEMS, define your MARKET with the JOBS TO BE DONE (JTBDs), not products

## Universal Job Steps Framework

**Core Concept:** All jobs, regardless of domain, follow the same universal pattern of 6 phases.

### The 6 Universal Phases

Every job follows these steps in sequence:

1. **UNDERSTAND** - Gather information needed to do the job
2. **PLAN** - Prepare for job execution
3. **EXECUTE** - Perform the core job activities
4. **ASSESS** - Evaluate progress and results
5. **REVISE** - Make adjustments based on assessment
6. **CONCLUDE** - Finalize and close out the job

### Example: "Get to Destinations On Time"

This job has **12 job steps** that map to the 6 universal phases:

**UNDERSTAND Phase:**
1. Estimate departure time
2. Determine what needs to be prepared

**PLAN Phase:**
3. Plan the stops
4. Set the departure time

**EXECUTE Phase:**
5. Get to the vehicle
6. Prepare the vehicle
7. Travel to the destination
8. Make planned stops

**ASSESS Phase:**
9. Assess when the destination will be reached

**REVISE Phase:**
10. Reset the route as needed

**CONCLUDE Phase:**
11. Park the vehicle
12. Walk to the destination

**Key Insight:** By breaking jobs into steps using this universal framework, we can systematically identify customer needs at each step.

## Customer Needs: The ACTION + VARIABLE Formula

### What is a Customer Need?

**Question:** "What is the CUSTOMER NEED?"

**Answer:** In order to execute a job step, a CUSTOMER NEEDS to take ACTIONS using VARIABLES in the JOB STEP.

**Formula:**
```
CUSTOMER NEED = ACTION (verb) + VARIABLE (noun)
```

### Why Customer Needs Are Stable

- PRODUCTS change over time
- JOBS TO BE DONE (JTBDs) are stable
- Therefore, CUSTOMER NEEDS are stable and do not change over time

### How to Generate Customer Needs

For each JOB STEP:
1. Identify the VARIABLES in that step (the things that need to be acted upon)
2. Identify the ACTIONS needed (the verbs describing what needs to be done)
3. Combine ACTION + VARIABLE to create the CUSTOMER NEED statement

### Example: "Plan the Stops" Job Step

**Job:** "Get to destinations on time"
**Job Step:** "Plan the stops" (a PLAN phase step)
**Variables in this step:** Optimal sequence, Routes, Open times, Wait times, Parking

**Customer Needs Generated:**

| ACTION | VARIABLE | CUSTOMER NEED |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Determine | Optimal Sequence | Determine optimal sequence of stops |
| Determine | Where to stop for errands | Determine where to stop for errands |
| Reduce | Total number of stops | Reduce the total number of stops |
| Determine | Routes to complete the errands | Determine routes to complete the errands |
| Determine | Additional time to complete errands | Determine the additional time to complete errands |
| Determine | Open times of locations | Determine open times for locations |
| Determine | Wait times at locations | Determine wait times at locations |
| Determine | Parking for errands | Determine parking for errands |

### Example: "Reset the Route As Needed" Job Step

**Job:** "Get to destinations on time"
**Job Step:** "Reset the route as needed" (a REVISE phase step)

**Customer Needs Generated:**

| ACTION | VARIABLE | CUSTOMER NEED |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Minimize | Time to identify route changes needed | Minimize time to identify route changes needed |
| Reduce | Number of route recalculations | Reduce the number of route recalculations |
| Determine | Alternative routes available | Determine alternative routes available |
| Minimize | Delay caused by route changes | Minimize delay caused by route changes |

## Comprehensive Need Collection

**Target:** Each job has between **80-100 CUSTOMER NEEDS**

**Why so many?**
- Jobs are complex with many steps
- Each step has multiple variables
- Each variable can have multiple actions
- Comprehensive coverage ensures no opportunities are missed

**Breakdown:**
- 8-15 job steps
- 5-10 needs per step on average
- Total: 80-100+ needs across the entire job

## Opportunity Scoring

### Importance and Satisfaction Ratings

For each CUSTOMER NEED, survey customers to rate:

**Importance:** "How important is it to [CUSTOMER NEED]?"
- Scale: 1 (Not at all important) to 10 (Extremely important)

**Satisfaction:** "How satisfied are you with your current ability to [CUSTOMER NEED]?"
- Scale: 1 (Not at all satisfied) to 10 (Completely satisfied)

### Opportunity Formula (Additive)

```
Opportunity Score = Importance + max(Importance - Satisfaction, 0)
```

**Interpretation:**
- **15-20:** Extremely underserved (HIGH OPPORTUNITY!)
- **10-15:** Underserved (significant opportunity)
- **5-10:** Appropriately served (table stakes, maintain parity)
- **0-5:** Overserved or irrelevant (don't invest here)

### Alternative: Multiplicative Formula

```
Opportunity Score = Importance × (Importance - Satisfaction)
```

**Interpretation:**
- **15+:** High opportunity
- **10-15:** Medium opportunity
- **<10:** Low opportunity

### What Opportunity Scores Reveal

**Underserved Needs (High Opportunity):**
- High importance to customers
- Low satisfaction with current solutions
- **These are your innovation opportunities**
- Invest here to differentiate and capture value

**Overserved Needs (Low Opportunity):**
- Low importance to customers
- High satisfaction with current solutions
- **These are feature bloat**
- Don't invest - customers don't care

**Appropriately Served (Table Stakes):**
- High importance to customers
- High satisfaction with current solutions
- **These are competitive necessities**
- Maintain parity but don't differentiate here

## Best Practices

### Job Statement Quality

**Good Job Statements:**
- ✅ Stable (doesn't change with technology)
- ✅ Outcome-focused (the result, not the solution)
- ✅ Action-oriented (uses a verb)
- ✅ Context-specific (includes qualifiers)

**Examples of Good Job Statements:**
- "Get to destinations on time"
- "Ensure adequate team coverage during absences"
- "Maintain visibility into project progress"
- "Minimize risk of data loss during system failures"

**Bad Job Statements:**
- ❌ "Use a navigation app" (solution-focused, not stable)
- ❌ "Manage employees" (too vague, no outcome)
- ❌ "Be productive" (not actionable, no context)

### Customer Need Quality

**Good Customer Needs:**
- ✅ Start with action verb
- ✅ Include specific variable
- ✅ Are outcome-focused
- ✅ Are measurable
- ✅ Are stable over time

**Examples of Good Needs:**
- "Minimize time to identify conflicting absences"
- "Determine alternative routes during congestion"
- "Reduce likelihood of coverage gaps during critical periods"

**Bad Customer Needs:**
- ❌ "Have a good app" (not specific, not measurable)
- ❌ "Use AI to predict" (solution-focused, not stable)
- ❌ "Get notifications" (feature, not need)

### Survey Best Practices

**Sample Size:**
- Minimum: 30 respondents (basic statistical significance)
- Good: 50+ respondents (reliable patterns)
- Excellent: 100+ respondents (detailed segmentation)

**Survey Design:**
- Ask for both importance AND satisfaction for each need
- Use consistent 1-10 scale
- Randomize need order to avoid bias
- Include demographic questions for segmentation
- Keep survey under 15 minutes to maintain quality responses

**Common Pitfalls:**
- ❌ Too few respondents (<30)
- ❌ Asking only importance (need satisfaction to calculate opportunity)
- ❌ Using inconsistent scales
- ❌ Surveying non-customers (they don't have the job)
- ❌ Mixing multiple jobs in one survey

## Integration with Other Methodologies

### ODI + Christensen's Forces

ODI identifies WHAT needs matter (importance × satisfaction gap).
Christensen identifies WHY people switch (push/pull/anxiety/habit).

**Combined insight:** Focus on high-opportunity needs (ODI) that have strong switching forces (Christensen).

### ODI + Economic Value to Customer

ODI identifies which needs are underserved.
EVC quantifies the dollar value of serving those needs better.

**Combined insight:** Prioritize opportunities with both high ODI scores AND high economic value.

### ODI + Multi-Dimensional Segmentation

ODI reveals which needs different segments prioritize.
Behavioral segmentation reveals observable patterns.

**Combined insight:** Target segments with the highest-opportunity needs AND observable behaviors you can action.

## Common Questions

**Q: How is ODI different from traditional feature prioritization?**
A: Traditional prioritization asks "What features should we build?" ODI asks "What jobs are underserved?" This leads to innovations, not just incremental features.

**Q: Why 80-100 needs? Isn't that too many?**
A: Jobs are complex. Missing needs means missing opportunities. Comprehensive coverage ensures nothing is overlooked. You'll prioritize later using opportunity scores.

**Q: Can I use ODI for B2C products?**
A: Yes! ODI works for any product where customers have a job to get done. B2C, B2B, physical products, software, services - all work.

**Q: What if customers can't articulate their needs?**
A: That's why we use job steps to systematically generate needs. Customers may not say "minimize time to identify conflicting absences," but you can infer it from the job step "identify upcoming absence requests."

**Q: How often should I re-survey customers?**
A: When the market changes significantly (new competitors, new technology) or annually to track trends. Needs are stable, but satisfaction levels change as solutions improve.

## Summary

**ODI Framework:**
1. Identify the main JOB TO BE DONE (stable, outcome-focused)
2. Decompose into JOB STEPS using Universal Job Steps framework (8-15 steps across 6 phases)
3. Generate CUSTOMER NEEDS using ACTION + VARIABLE formula (80-100 needs)
4. Survey customers for IMPORTANCE and SATISFACTION ratings
5. Calculate OPPORTUNITY SCORES (Importance + max(Importance - Satisfaction, 0))
6. Prioritize underserved needs (high opportunity scores)
7. Build solutions that address those needs

**Why ODI Works:**
- Focuses on stable jobs, not changing products
- Systematic and comprehensive (nothing is missed)
- Data-driven (importance and satisfaction ratings)
- Reveals hidden opportunities (high importance, low satisfaction)
- Applicable across all industries and product types

**Next Steps:**
- Learn the Universal Job Steps framework in detail
- Practice generating needs with ACTION + VARIABLE formula
- Study real examples to internalize the approach
- Apply ODI to your own product/market
