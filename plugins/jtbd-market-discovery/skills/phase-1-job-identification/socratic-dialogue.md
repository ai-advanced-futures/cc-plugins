# Phase 1: Job Identification

**Goal:** Define the main job customers are trying to accomplish
**Duration:** 30-45 minutes
**Output:** Validated job statement

---

## What Happens in This Phase

In Phase 1, we'll work together to identify and validate the main **Job To Be Done (JTBD)** for your market. This is the foundation for everything that follows.

**Key Activities:**
1. Identify your target market or product area
2. Discover what customers are trying to accomplish (the outcome, not the solution)
3. Draft a job statement using the proper format
4. Validate the job statement against quality criteria
5. (Optional) Validate with customers if you have access

---

## Core Principle: Customers Hire Products

**Remember:** Customers hire products to get a job done.

**Examples:**

| Product (Changes) | Job To Be Done (Stable) |
|-------------------|-------------------------|
| Quarter-inch drill | Create a quarter-inch hole |
| Paper maps → GPS → smartphone apps | Get to destinations on time |
| Spreadsheets → HR software | Keep a covering person for each absence |
| Status meetings → PM tools → dashboards | Maintain visibility into project progress |

**Why This Matters:**
- Products are temporary solutions that eventually become obsolete
- Jobs To Be Done are stable and stay the same when technology changes
- When you define your market by the JOB (not the product), you unlock innovation opportunities

If you're stuck, I'll ask:

- "If all current solutions disappeared, what would they still need to accomplish?"

## Step 2: Draft Your Job Statement

Once we've identified the outcome, I'll help you structure it properly:

### Job Statement Formula

```
[ACTION VERB] + [OBJECT OF ACTION] + [CONTEXTUAL CLARIFIER]
```

### Quality Criteria

A good job statement is:

1. **Stable**
   - Would this job exist 10 years ago?
   - Will it exist 10 years from now?
   - ✅ "Get to destinations on time" (existed for centuries)
   - ❌ "Use GPS navigation" (specific to current technology)

2. **Outcome-Focused**
   - Does it describe a result, not a solution?
   - ✅ "Maintain visibility into project progress" (result)
   - ❌ "Use a project management dashboard" (solution)

3. **Action-Oriented**
   - Does it start with an action verb?
   - ✅ "Keep a covering person for each absence" (verb = ensure)
   - ❌ "Team productivity" (no verb, too vague)

4. **Context-Specific**
   - Does it include qualifiers that narrow the scope?
   - ✅ "Identify blockers before they impact timelines" (context = before impact)
   - ❌ "Identify blockers" (too broad, missing context)

5. **Solution-Agnostic**
   - Could multiple different products/approaches address this?
   - ✅ "Track project progress against deadlines" (many solutions possible)
   - ❌ "Update Jira tickets daily" (specific solution)

### Validation Checklist

I'll walk you through validation:

```
Claude: Let's validate your job statement against the quality criteria:

        Your job: "Identify and resolve team blockers before they impact timelines"

        ✅ Stable: This job existed before modern software (managers always needed this)
        ✅ Outcome-focused: Describes result (blockers resolved), not solution
        ✅ Action-oriented: Starts with "identify" and "resolve"
        ✅ Context-specific: "before they impact timelines" provides important context
        ✅ Solution-agnostic: Could use meetings, tools, 1-on-1s, async updates, etc.

        This is a high-quality job statement! ✅
```

## Step 4: Common Pitfalls

I'll help you avoid these traps:

### Pitfall 1: Too Broad

**Bad:** "Be successful in business"
**Why:** Not actionable, no clear outcome, unmeasurable

**Good:** "Maintain visibility into team productivity during remote work"
**Why:** Specific outcome, clear context, measurable

### Pitfall 2: Solution-Focused

**Bad:** "Use project management software"
**Why:** References specific solution, will become obsolete

**Good:** "Track project progress against deadlines"
**Why:** Outcome-focused, stable, solution-agnostic

### Pitfall 3: Too Granular

**Bad:** "Click the save button to preserve work"
**Why:** Too specific, task-level (not job-level)

**Good:** "Preserve work progress to avoid data loss"
**Why:** Job-level outcome, stable need

### Pitfall 4: Multiple Jobs Combined

**Bad:** "Plan trips and book hotels and rent cars"
**Why:** Three separate jobs bundled together

**Options:**
- Focus on one: "Arrange travel logistics for business trips"
- Or treat as separate jobs to study individually

**Good:** "Arrange travel logistics for business trips"
**Why:** Single job statement, clear scope

### Pitfall 5: Feature-Focused

**Bad:** "Get push notifications about tasks"
**Why:** Describes a feature, not an outcome

**Good:** "Stay aware of upcoming deadlines without constant checking"
**Why:** Outcome-focused, explains the why behind notifications

## Step 5: Customer Validation (Optional)

If you have access to customers, I'll guide you through validation:

### Validation Questions

**1. Resonance Check:**
```
"When I say [JOB STATEMENT], does that resonate with you?
Does it describe something you need to do?"
```

**2. Importance:**
```
"On a scale of 1-10, how important is it to [JOB STATEMENT]?"
```

**3. Frequency:**
```
"How often do you need to [JOB STATEMENT]?
Daily? Weekly? Monthly?"
```

**4. Consequence:**
```
"What happens if you can't [JOB STATEMENT] well?
What's the impact?"
```

### Validation Example

```
Claude: Let's validate with a few customers. I'll provide a script:

        Interview Script:
        "I'm studying how engineering managers identify and resolve
        team blockers. When I say 'identify and resolve team blockers
        before they impact timelines,' does that resonate with you?"

        Expected responses:
        ✅ "Yes, that's exactly what I spend half my time doing"
        ✅ "Absolutely, that's critical to my role"
        ⚠️  "Sort of, but I'd say it's more about preventing blockers"
        ❌ "Not really, I mostly focus on hiring"

        If you get mostly ✅ responses, your job statement is validated!
        If you get ⚠️ or ❌, we need to refine.

        What feedback did you get from customers?
```

### Red Flags

Watch for these warning signs:
- **"Sort of..."** - Job statement too vague or doesn't fully capture the need
- **"Not really..."** - Wrong job entirely, need to restart
- **"I don't do that job"** - Wrong target customer segment
- **"That's not important"** - Low-importance job; study it later

## Integration with Forces Analysis

At this stage, I may also introduce **Christensen's Forces**:

```
Claude: Now that we have the job, let's understand WHY customers
        might switch from their current solution.

        Christensen's Four Forces of Progress:

        1. PUSH: What problems with the current solution push
           customers to look for alternatives?

        2. PULL: What would attract customers to a new solution?

        3. ANXIETY: What concerns might prevent customers from switching?

        4. HABIT: What makes customers comfortable with the status quo?

        Let's explore each force. First, what PUSHES customers away
        from their current approach to [JOB]?
```

### Example Forces Dialogue

```
You: Our job is "Identify and resolve team blockers before they impact timelines"

Claude: What PUSHES engineering managers away from their current
        approach to identifying blockers?

You: They find out about blockers too late, usually in standups

Claude: Good! So one PUSH force is: "Delayed blocker discovery"

        What else frustrates them about the current approach?

You: They spend too much time in status meetings asking everyone

Claude: Excellent. Another PUSH: "Time-intensive status gathering"

        Now for PULL - what would attract them to a new solution?

You: Real-time visibility without having to ask

Claude: Perfect. PULL force: "Automatic blocker detection"

        For ANXIETY - what would make them hesitant to adopt something new?

You: Worried it would be another tool nobody uses

Claude: Key ANXIETY: "Tool adoption risk"

        And for HABIT - what keeps them using the current approach?

You: Status meetings are familiar and predictable

Claude: HABIT force: "Comfort with meeting-based updates"

        Great! Let me calculate switch likelihood:
        PUSH (7/10) + PULL (8/10) - ANXIETY (5/10) - HABIT (4/10) = +6

        Interpretation: Likely to switch with moderate effort needed
        to reduce ANXIETY and HABIT forces.
```

## Tips for Success in Phase 1

### Do's

✅ **Focus on outcomes, not solutions**
- Think about WHAT customers want to accomplish, not HOW they do it today

✅ **Be specific with context**
- "Get to destinations" → "Get to destinations on time"
- Context makes the job statement actionable

✅ **Validate with customers early**
- 5-10 conversations can confirm or redirect your thinking
- Better to pivot now than after building a survey

✅ **Use examples as reference**
- Navigation, team coverage, project visibility examples are in the templates
- Study them to internalize what good looks like

### Don'ts

**Keep specific products and technologies out of the job statement**
- "Use Jira" → solution-focused, will become obsolete
- "Track project progress" → outcome-focused, stable

**Keep one job in each statement**
- "Plan trips and find restaurants" → two separate jobs
- Focus on one job per discovery process

**Validate each job statement**
- Your assumptions about customer jobs may be wrong
- 30 minutes of validation saves weeks of wasted work

**Accept a first job statement and refine it later**
- You can refine the job statement as you learn more
- Good enough now, perfect later

## Common Questions

**Q: What if I have multiple customer segments?**

A: Start with one segment, complete the full process, then repeat for other segments. Different segments may have different jobs or prioritize different needs for the same job.

**Q: What if customers can't articulate their job?**

A: That's normal! Use the Socratic questions I provide to guide them. Focus on outcomes, consequences, and what they're trying to accomplish.

**Q: Can I study multiple jobs at once?**

A: No. Each job should be studied separately. You'll generate 80-100 needs per job - that's enough complexity for one analysis.

**Q: What if the job I identify is too broad?**

A: We'll narrow it during validation. If it takes more than 15 steps to decompose, it's probably multiple jobs bundled together.

**Q: Do I need customer access for Phase 1?**

A: Validation is strongly recommended but not required. You can proceed with your best hypothesis and validate later with survey data.
