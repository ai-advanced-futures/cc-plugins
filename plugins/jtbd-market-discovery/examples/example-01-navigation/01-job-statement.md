# Job Statement: Get to Destinations on Time

## Complete Job Statement

**When** I need to travel to a destination with a specific arrival time requirement,

**I want to** navigate efficiently using optimal routes while adapting to real-time conditions,

**So I can** arrive on time consistently and minimize stress from uncertainty.

---

## Job Components

### Job Performer
**Urban Commuter** - Individual who regularly travels to time-sensitive destinations in metropolitan areas

**Characteristics:**
- Professional with scheduled meetings/appointments
- Parent with school pickup/dropoff responsibilities
- Healthcare worker with shift requirements
- Student with class schedules
- Anyone with time-dependent commitments

**NOT the performer:**
- Delivery drivers (different job: "Deliver packages efficiently")
- Taxi/rideshare drivers (different job: "Transport passengers safely")
- Tourists (different job: "Explore new areas")
- Emergency responders (different job: "Reach emergencies quickly")

### Desired Outcome
**Arrive on time consistently** - The core objective is punctuality, not just navigation

Secondary outcomes:
- Minimize travel time
- Reduce stress and anxiety
- Maximize predictability
- Avoid traffic delays
- Minimize fuel costs

### Job Context & Circumstances

**When does this job arise?**
- Daily commute to office (weekdays, 7-9 AM, 5-7 PM)
- Important meetings or appointments
- School pickup/dropoff (time-critical)
- Medical appointments
- Flight/train departure deadlines
- Social commitments (dinners, events)

**What triggers this job?**
- Calendar notification for upcoming event
- Need to leave current location
- Time pressure realization
- Uncertain route conditions
- Unfamiliar destination

**Why is the job important?**
- Professional consequences (missed meetings, late to work)
- Personal stress (anxiety from uncertainty)
- Social implications (letting others down)
- Financial costs (late fees, lost opportunities)
- Reputation impact (perceived as unreliable)

**Environmental factors:**
- Urban environment (high traffic density)
- Time of day (rush hour vs off-peak)
- Weather conditions (rain, snow, fog)
- Road construction/accidents
- Special events (concerts, sports, parades)
- GPS accuracy/availability

## Forces Diagram

The forces diagram captures what pushes someone to switch from their current solution and what holds them back.

### 🔴 Push Forces (Problems with Current Solution)

These are the pains and frustrations that motivate someone to seek a better solution:

1. **Frequent late arrivals despite planning**
   - "I left 'on time' but still arrived 10 minutes late"
   - "Traffic was worse than Google Maps predicted"
   - Current tools underestimate travel time 20-30% of the time

2. **High stress from time uncertainty**
   - "I never know if I should leave now or wait 5 more minutes"
   - Constant anxiety checking multiple apps for updates
   - Mental burden of contingency planning

3. **Poor real-time adaptation**
   - "My route had an accident but the app didn't reroute me"
   - Missed turns because of delayed notifications
   - Stuck in traffic with no alternative suggested

4. **Inaccurate departure time estimates**
   - "It says 25 minutes but I need to figure out parking too"
   - Doesn't account for destination-specific factors
   - No learning from past trips to same destination

5. **Wasted time from inefficient routes**
   - "This route added 15 minutes but saved me $2 in tolls"
   - Free apps optimize for distance, not time value
   - No customization for personal preferences

**Intensity:** High for professionals, parents, healthcare workers (Crisis Avoiders segment)

---

### 🟢 Pull Forces (Benefits of New Solution)

These are the compelling benefits that attract someone to switch:

1. **Reliable arrival time predictions**
   - "It says I'll arrive at 2:47 PM and I actually do"
   - Confidence in estimates based on historical accuracy
   - Machine learning from millions of trips

2. **Proactive real-time alerts**
   - "Accident ahead - rerouting you now"
   - Notifications before problems become delays
   - Alternative routes pre-calculated

3. **Smart departure time estimation**
   - "Leave by 2:15 PM to arrive on time"
   - Accounts for parking, walking, destination specifics
   - Learns from your past trips and preferences

4. **Stress reduction through certainty**
   - "You're on track - relax"
   - Visual timeline showing buffer/cushion
   - Peace of mind worth paying for

5. **Time savings optimization**
   - Routes optimized for time value ($/hour)
   - "Save 12 minutes for $3 toll - worth it?"
   - Personalized cost-benefit analysis

6. **Premium positioning**
   - "Professional-grade navigation"
   - Status signal (like Global Entry for travel)
   - Worth it for those who value their time

**Intensity:** Very high for Crisis Avoiders (acute pain), moderate for Balanced Users

---

### 🟡 Anxiety Forces (Worries About Switching)

These are the concerns that make someone hesitate to adopt a new solution:

1. **Cost concerns**
   - "Google Maps is free - why pay $69/month?"
   - Sticker shock compared to free alternatives
   - ROI uncertainty ("Will I really save enough time?")

2. **Switching costs**
   - "I'm used to Google Maps interface"
   - Muscle memory with current app
   - Learning curve for new tool

3. **Privacy concerns**
   - "What data are they collecting about my locations?"
   - Trust in location tracking
   - Third-party data sharing policies

4. **Reliability doubts**
   - "What if it's wrong and I'm even later?"
   - Risk of trying something new for important trips
   - "I'll test it for low-stakes trips first"

5. **Feature parity concerns**
   - "Does it have everything Google Maps has?"
   - Missing features (bike routes, transit, walking)
   - Integration with calendar, contacts

6. **Battery and data usage**
   - "Will it drain my battery faster?"
   - Cellular data consumption
   - Need for constant connectivity

**Intensity:** High initially, reduces after trial period and positive experiences

---

### 🔵 Habit Forces (Inertia of Current Behavior)

These are the entrenched habits that keep someone using their current solution:

1. **Default app on phone**
   - Google Maps pre-installed on Android
   - Apple Maps default on iOS
   - Siri/Google Assistant integration

2. **Social proof and familiarity**
   - "Everyone uses Google Maps/Waze"
   - Network effects (friends share locations)
   - Comfort with familiar interface

3. **"Good enough" mentality**
   - "I'm usually on time with current approach"
   - Loss aversion (fear of worse outcomes)
   - "If it ain't broke, don't fix it"

4. **Embedded in workflow**
   - Calendar auto-adds Google Maps links
   - Waze integrated with music/podcasts
   - Existing app ecosystem dependencies

5. **Free tier satisfaction**
   - "Why pay when free works most of the time?"
   - Haven't experienced acute pain recently
   - Discount future costs of being late

6. **Cognitive inertia**
   - "I don't want to think about this"
   - Navigation is background task
   - Not top-of-mind problem

**Intensity:** Very high for Satisfied Users, moderate for Balanced Users, low for Crisis Avoiders

## Job Success Criteria

How do we know when the job is done well?

### Objective Measures
- ✅ Arrive within 5 minutes of predicted time (95% of trips)
- ✅ Zero missed meetings/appointments due to navigation
- ✅ Reduce late arrivals by 80% vs current baseline
- ✅ Save 15+ minutes per trip on average

### Subjective Measures
- ✅ Feel confident about departure time decisions
- ✅ Experience reduced stress during travel
- ✅ Trust the app's predictions completely
- ✅ Willing to rely on app for high-stakes trips

### Economic Measures
- ✅ Save $750+/month in time value
- ✅ Avoid $150+/month in late arrival costs
- ✅ 3-5x ROI on subscription cost
- ✅ Worth paying premium over free alternatives

## Job Statement Evolution

### Initial (Too Vague)
❌ "Navigate to places"

**Problems:**
- No outcome specified
- No context provided
- Could apply to tourists, drivers, everyone

### Improved (Adding Context)
⚠️ "Get to work on time every day"

**Problems:**
- Too specific (only work commute)
- Misses other time-sensitive trips
- Limits market size artificially

### Final (Outcome-Focused)
✅ "Get to destinations on time"

**Why this works:**
- Clear outcome (on time)
- Broad context (all destinations)
- Measurable success
- Applies to target market
- Solution-agnostic
