## What is a Job Statement?

A **job statement** describes what your customers are trying to accomplish. It's stable over time and doesn't reference specific solutions or products.

**Formula:**
```
[ACTION VERB] + [OBJECT OF ACTION] + [CONTEXTUAL CLARIFIER]
```

**Good Examples:**
- "Get to destinations on time"
- "Ensure adequate team coverage during absences"
- "Maintain visibility into project progress"
- "Minimize risk of data loss during system failures"

**Bad Examples:**
- ❌ "Use a navigation app" (solution-focused, not stable)
- ❌ "Manage employees" (too vague, no outcome)
- ❌ "Be productive" (not actionable, no context)

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### ❌ Pitfall 1: Too Broad
**Bad:** "Be successful in business"
**Good:** "Maintain visibility into team productivity during remote work"

### ❌ Pitfall 2: Solution-Focused
**Bad:** "Use project management software"
**Good:** "Track project progress against deadlines"

### ❌ Pitfall 3: Too Granular
**Bad:** "Click the save button"
**Good:** "Preserve work progress to avoid data loss"

### ❌ Pitfall 4: Multiple Jobs Combined
**Bad:** "Plan trips and book hotels and rent cars"
**Good:** "Arrange travel logistics for business trips" (or break into 3 separate jobs)

### ❌ Pitfall 5: Feature-Focused
**Bad:** "Get push notifications about tasks"
**Good:** "Stay aware of upcoming deadlines without constant checking"

## Examples from Real Projects

### Example 1: Navigation Job

**Market:** Daily commuters and travelers
**Job Statement:** "Get to destinations on time"

**Why It's Good:**
- Stable (existed before GPS, will exist after)
- Outcome-focused (result = arrive on time)
- Action-oriented (verb = get to)
- Context-specific (qualifier = on time)
- Solution-agnostic (maps, GPS, public transit schedules all address this)

**Validation:**
- Importance: 8.9/10 average across 100 respondents
- Frequency: Daily for 73% of respondents
- Alternative phrasings tested: "Navigate to places" (less specific), "Avoid being late to appointments" (negative framing)

### Example 2: Team Coverage Job

**Market:** Team leads and managers in knowledge work
**Job Statement:** "Ensure adequate team coverage during absences"

**Why It's Good:**
- Stable (existed before software tools, will exist after)
- Outcome-focused (result = adequate coverage)
- Action-oriented (verb = ensure)
- Context-specific (qualifier = during absences)
- Solution-agnostic (spreadsheets, HR software, manual planning all address this)

**Validation:**
- Importance: 9.1/10 average across 50 managers
- Frequency: Weekly for 68% of respondents
- Alternative phrasings tested: "Manage vacation requests" (solution-focused), "Maintain productivity when people are out" (less specific)

### Example 3: Project Visibility Job

**Market:** Project managers and executives
**Job Statement:** "Maintain visibility into project progress"

**Why It's Good:**
- Stable (fundamental management need)
- Outcome-focused (result = visibility)
- Action-oriented (verb = maintain)
- Context-specific (qualifier = into project progress)
- Solution-agnostic (status meetings, dashboards, reports all address this)

**Validation:**
- Importance: 9.3/10 average across 75 PMs
- Frequency: Daily for 81% of respondents
- Alternative phrasings tested: "Track projects" (too vague), "Use project management tools" (solution-focused)
