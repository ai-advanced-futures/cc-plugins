# Ask the knowledge graphs

The question is: **$ARGUMENTS**

Answer from the graphs only. Never write a page, because an ask only reads.

`<K>` is `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/kq.py`. Write it in full in each
Bash call. Do not add `| head`, `2>&1` or a second command to it: the output
of each command is short. Do not read the files of the graph with Read, Grep,
`cat` or `grep`.

## Step 1: the first search

The script searched the graphs with the words of the question before you read
this. These are the results.

```!
python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/kq.py find --stdin <<'KQ_END_OF_QUESTION'
$ARGUMENTS
KQ_END_OF_QUESTION
```

For each page, the result gives one `==` line with its node, type, title and
status, then its description and its text (`>` lines). The 8 best pages are
printed almost in full. After them come the best passages of other pages.

## Step 2: search with the words of the graph

The first search uses the words of the question as they are. The pages can use
other words for the same thing. Do this step unless the results above state
each part of the answer in plain words. Run:

    <K> find "<words>" "<words>" "<words>" "<words>"

Give 3 or 4 searches of 3 to 8 words each. Use the words that a page would
use: a name, a title, a role, a term that you saw in the results, a synonym.
Make the searches different from each other.

You have the answer only when a page states each part of it in plain words. A
page that is only near the subject is not the answer. A page that says the
thing is not known, not decided or still open is an answer: report what it
says. When the answer is not
there, run the command one more time with other words. Stop rule: run 2 `find`
commands or fewer in this step.

## Step 3: the walk from the answer

When you have the answer, run this command one time. Do not skip this step:
the answer alone is not enough.

    <K> near <node> <node> --q "<the words of the answer>"

- `<node>`: the page that holds the answer, and a page that led you to it.
  Give 3 pages or fewer.
- `--q`: the names, the dates and the terms of the answer.

The script walks along the links from those pages and prints the pages that
the walk comes to most. The context of an answer is on those pages. When you
have no answer after step 2, run the command with the 2 nearest pages.

## Step 4: answer

Write the answer for your user. Use only the text that the tool gave you. Do
not add a fact from another place. When the text does not hold the answer, say
so and do not guess.

Use this form. Plain text only: no heading, no bold text, no table, no page
path. Do not write a sentence before the answer or a summary after the last
line.

1. The answer, in 2 to 5 sentences. State each thing that the question asks
   for, with its name, date or number. When the text says that a thing is
   not defined, not decided, not known or in dispute, say that in the first
   sentence, with the words of the text. Do not state as settled a thing
   that the text leaves open. When the text says that a thing is absent or
   was left out, say that it is absent; do not say only that it is open. Then give the sentence of the page that
   states the answer, with the words and the numbers of the page. Do not
   rewrite it. When the answer is that a thing is not known or not recorded,
   give also the sentence that says what is known in its place.
2. The line `Context:`.
3. 8 to 10 lines that start with `- `. Each line is one sentence of 16 words
   or fewer. Each line states one fact that changes how a reader uses the
   answer. Most of these facts are on pages other than the page of the
   answer. Look for these six kinds of fact:

   - a source that disagrees with the answer;
   - a later correction of the answer, or a newer value;
   - a weak point of the source of the answer: one source only, not
     confirmed, partial;
   - a related question that is still open;
   - a decision or a condition that the answer depends on;
   - a consequence of the answer for another thing.

State the fact itself, with its name, date or number. A line that only says
that a page exists, or what a page is about, is of no use. When a line gives
a value that is different from the answer, name the source of that value. Say
which value is the current one only when the text says so. Do not write a
line for a kind that the text does not hold.
